# Vendora public-site wireframe review

## Task

Review a proposed low-fidelity wireframe for Vendora's pre-login experience. The product owner asked to start with the landing page and make clear that sign-up/login leads to the merchant dashboard. Give an independent UX/content critique before Codex finalizes the wireframe.

## Product evidence

- Vendora is a working-name merchant workspace for conversations, customers, catalog/stock, orders, fulfilment, AI assistance, and voice stock administration.
- Assumed early persona: owner-operated apparel/accessories sellers with one to five staff, selling mainly through Instagram and WhatsApp.
- Ranked pain hypothesis: customer context, variant stock, chats, and order state are fragmented, producing missed follow-ups and fulfilment mistakes.
- Product promise must not imply that live WhatsApp, payments, AI providers, pricing, or production deployment already exist.
- Pricing, provider choices, testimonials, traction, quantified outcomes, domain/trademark, and launch dates are not approved.
- After successful sign-up/login, the intended destination is the merchant dashboard. The dashboard itself is not part of this task.

## Proposed public routes

- `/` landing page.
- `/product` deeper product/how-it-works page.
- `/pricing` plan structure or pilot invitation without unapproved numeric prices.
- `/login`, `/signup`, `/forgot-password` authentication entry screens.
- `/privacy` and `/terms` placeholders must not be presented as legally complete until reviewed.

## Proposed landing-page structure

1. Header: Vendora wordmark; Product, How it works, Pricing; Log in; primary `Start free`/`Join pilot` CTA.
2. Hero: outcome-led headline about turning conversations into trackable orders; support text; primary sign-up CTA; secondary product-tour anchor.
3. Product preview: one coherent mock showing conversation, customer, order, and stock—not invented analytics.
4. Problem-to-workflow section: chats arrive → catalog/stock checked → order tracked → fulfilment handed off.
5. Three capability sections: shared customer context; correct catalog/stock/order workflow; AI/voice assistance with explicit review/confirmation.
6. Trust/safety strip: human handover, verified payment events, role/tenant controls; phrase as product principles, not shipped claims.
7. “Built for social sellers” segment framing without unsupported customer logos or testimonials.
8. Final CTA and minimal footer.

## Design alternatives under consideration

- **Operations-first (recommended candidate):** hero emphasizes “Turn every conversation into a trackable order” and shows the entire conversation-to-order workflow.
- **Conversation-first:** hero emphasizes a unified selling inbox and moves the operational workflow below it.

## Questions

1. Which direction better matches the stated job-to-be-done and why?
2. What is missing, misleading, redundant, or too implementation-specific?
3. What should the exact primary/secondary CTA wording be while the product is not yet live?
4. Which public routes are truly needed for the first pre-login wireframe, and which can be deferred?
5. Give a recommended section order, with one-sentence purpose per section.
6. Identify any claims that must be labelled proposed or removed.

Return concise recommendations only. Do not claim to inspect files, run tests, or edit the repository. Do not provide hidden chain-of-thought.
